# Compline under the rubrics of Divino Afflatu (pre-1955): plan

For [issue #13](https://github.com/bbloomf/compline/issues/13). Status: Phase 1 done (2026-10-28); Phases 2, 3 and 4 done (2026-10-29). What's left: the regional calendars (Phase 2).

The office as it stood from Pius X's reform (Divino Afflatu, 1911) until the simplification of 1955 (*Cum nostra hac aetate*). Divinum Officium (DO), the source of the translations, has this as its "Divino Afflatu - 1954" version, which can serve as the reference throughout.

## Phase 1: the choice, and what doesn't depend on the day (done)

- `#selectRubrics`, next to the date: "1960 rubrics" (`1960`) or "Divino Afflatu (pre-1955)" (`DA`), kept in `localStorage.rubrics`. `calendar.setRubrics` keeps the choice, and `include='divinoAfflatu'` (or `exclude=`) shows a part of `index.html` only under one or the other.
- After *Adjutorium nostrum*: the *Pater noster* said silently throughout (`pater-silent`), where 1960 has the examination of conscience (`examen`). DO's `Ordinarium/Completorium.txt` shows `$rubrica examen` only for 1960.
- After *Divinum auxilium*: the *Pater noster*, *Ave Maria* and *Credo*, said silently, and the rubric for when Matins follows (`pater-ave-credo`, `pater-ave-credo-matins`). DO drops these for 1955 and 1960. The texts come from DO's `Prayers.txt` (`index:pater-noster`, `index:ave-maria`, `index:credo` in `tools/do-import/manifest.json`). The rubrics are drafted in each language's `extra/<lang>/rubrics.txt` for the native readers to check.
- Until Phase 4, a note under the date said what didn't follow Divino Afflatu yet. Phase 4 removed it: only the regional calendars are left, which are documented under Phase 2.

## Phase 2: the calendar (done)

Rather than rebuild DO's occurrence, transfers, octaves and concurrence in JavaScript, the app takes them from DO's own engine. The result is a table, so the app still works offline.

- `tools/do-import/da-calendar/compline-office.pl` loads DO's Perl (`horascommon.pl` and the rest, with `cgi-stub/` standing in for `CGI.pm`). For each day it calls DO's `precedence` with the hour set to Compline, so concurrence is applied. It prints the office that Compline follows: DO's file, the Latin and English titles, the rank, `duplex`, whether these are the office's First or Second Vespers, and the psalms (`psalmi_minor`).
- A year's calendar depends only on its Easter and on whether it's a leap year. That gives 70 kinds of year, and 69 of them occur between 1583 and 4099. `tools/do-import/da-calendar/build.js` (`npm run build-da-calendar`) runs the script over one year of each kind and writes `js/calendar-da.js`, about 115 KB (16 KB gzipped). The file holds:
  - 519 offices, each with its key, English title, *ol* for Our Lady, and Sunday's psalms on another day;
  - 19 ranks, each with its English name, `duplex`, a 1960-style class for the existing date tokens, and *sunday*;
  - three characters a day for each kind of year.
  The build translates the titles of the temporal cycle, which DO's English files leave in Latin, into English in `calendar.js`'s style. It also translates the rank names ("Double of the I Class", "Lesser Sunday").
- `calendar.js` loads the table only when Divino Afflatu is chosen (`setRubrics(r, callback)`). `getFeastForDate` then returns the office that Compline follows, with `rankName`, `duplex`, `first` (First Vespers) and `psalms`. The feast line shows, for example, "All Saints (Double of the I Class, First Vespers)" on 31 October.
  - `rank` maps the office to 1960's classes, so the existing tokens (`firstClassFeast`, `feria` and the rest) still work: I and II class are 1 and 2, the other feasts and octave days 3, simples 4, ferias 10.
  - Under DA, `allSouls` is the evening before All Souls, as in DO: its Compline follows the First Vespers of the Dead.
- **The psalms** come from DO too: Sunday's where DO says them, as on doubles of the I and II class and in the octaves of Easter, the Ascension, Pentecost and Corpus Christi; elsewhere the weekday's. On Saturday evening before a Sunday they're Saturday's.
- **Checked:** for 1950, 2025, 2027, 2028 and 2100, none of which built the table, the app's lookup in node matches DO day by day: 1,826 days, with no differences in the office, its First or Second Vespers, or `duplex`. That includes 2025, when All Souls is moved off a Sunday.
- **Regional calendars:** only the universal calendar for now, and under DA the app ignores the region choice and its alternates. Looked into on 2026-10-29; left for later:
  - **No source:** DO's diocesan calendars (`dioecesis`) are Rome's, a few German and Dutch dioceses' and Brasília's, none of the app's regions'. restorethe54's ordo is universal only. No machine-readable pre-1955 national proper turned up for the US, Canada, England, Wales, Scotland or Australia and New Zealand, so one would have to be transcribed from a printed Ordo of the time.
  - **calendar.js's regional lists can't be reused:** they're 1960's, and some of their feasts came later: St. Elizabeth Ann Seton and St. Oliver Plunkett (canonized 1975), St. John Ogilvie (1976), the US's 22 January. Their ranks are 1960's classes, not doubles.
  - **More than a title:** under DA a proper feast changes the occurrence, the transfers and the concurrence around it, and so which Compline is said. It would go through DO's engine: a kalendarium of the region, and a stub office with each feast's rank, giving a table of its own for each region.

## Phase 3: what follows from the day (done)

DO's reference here is its whole Compline for the day (`specials()` on its `Ordinarium/Completorium.txt`), not only its calendar. `compline-office.pl` prints three more columns, which `build.js` carries into `js/calendar-da.js` (now four characters a day) and `getDAFeast` returns as `preces` and `hymn`. It also reads DO's files afresh each day, as each of DO's pages does: DO resolves a file's conditions (the hymn's `tonus` and `tempore`) when it first reads it. The office columns are unchanged by that.

- **The psalms label:** "Sunday at Compline (Feast or Octave with Sunday's Psalms)" under DA. DO gives Sunday's psalms on the doubles of the I and II class, on many greater doubles (feasts of Our Lord, Our Lady, the Angels and the Apostles), and in the privileged octaves.
- **Holy Saturday:** DO says Compline after Easter's First Vespers, so under DA `isTriduum` ends with Good Friday, and `index.html` reads the tokens `triduum` and `triduumSaturday` where it had `easter-3:easter-1` and `easter-1`. As in the Easter octave, Sunday's psalms have no antiphon, and there's no hymn, chapter or responsory. The *Nunc dimittis* has the Magnificat's antiphon, *Vespere autem sabbati* (DO's `Tempora/Pasc0-0`, `[Ant 41]`, with its GABC from DO's Latin-gabc). It's in mode 8 G, so the canticle is too (`canticle-psalm-hs`, made from the Easter octave's, which is pointed the same way). *Deus in adjutorium* ends with *Alleluia*, and the Marian antiphon is *Regina caeli*.
- **The antiphon:** the plan had it doubled on doubles, but DO, as the rubrics before 1955, doubles the antiphons only at Matins, Lauds and Vespers (`antetpsalm`'s `$duplexf`). At Compline, on every day, only the antiphon's opening words, up to its \*, are said before the psalms, and the whole of it after them; the same for *Salva nos* at the *Nunc dimittis*. So under DA each `psalms/<day>/ant.gabc`, `psalms/ant-PT.gabc` ("Alleluia."), `canticle-ant.gabc` and `canticle-ant-hs.gabc` has its `-intonation.gabc`. The manifest's `intonation` cuts the translations the same way. The Easter octave's alleluias and All Souls' Day are as before.
- **The preces:** DO's `preces('Dominicales')`: 0, 1, or 2 where DO adds "flexis genibus" (where the Feriales are said at the other hours: the ferias of Advent and Lent, Ember days and the vigils). After the canticle's antiphon (`include='preces'`): the *Kyrie*, the *Pater noster* and *Credo* said silently up to their last verses, the *Preces dominicales Completorium*, and *Domine exaudi*, which then isn't said again before the collect when no priest is present (DO's `Dominus_vobiscum`). The texts are DO's `Prayers.txt` and `Psalterium/Special/Preces.txt` (`index:kyrie`, `index:preces-pater`, `index:preces-credo`, `index:preces-dominicales`). The rubrics `preces`, `preces-kneeling` and `preces-credo` are drafted in each language's `extra/<lang>/rubrics.txt` for the native readers to check.
- **The hymn:** under DA, the tone and the doxology are DO's for each day, and the `select-date` rules of 1960 are skipped. The tone is DO's choice from Latin-gabc's `[Hymnus Completorium]` (by its chant tone and season), mapped to the app's melodies; the doxology is DO's `doxology()`. Eleven of the pairs DO gives have no file of their own in 1960 (twelve until Phase 4's fix dropped `Ascension-Easter`), so `te-lucis/<Melody>-<Doxology>.gabc` sets one tone's doxology to another's melody (`Advent-Christmas` in the octave of the Immaculate Conception, for example), and `Our-Lady-Seven-Sorrows` is Our Lady's melody with the Seven Sorrows' own doxology (DO's `Tempora/Quad5-5`). They're listed in `build.js`'s `HYMNS`, and in the hymn's menu only under DA; the build stops if a day's hymn has no file or menu item. One choice of DO's isn't followed: its Paschal melody (`in_tempore_paschali--`, with no source, the Antiphonale Monasticum's) isn't the Liber Usualis's, which is kept, as the Liber Usualis of 1932 has it too. Phase 4 looked again at the pairs that looked like DO's quirks: the Christmas doxology from a commemorated Office of Our Lady is followed, and the seasons of a few evenings aren't (Phase 4). English and Spanish can't sing the Seven Sorrows' doxology yet, so it's shown as text there; English has a literal translation of it, written for the app.
- **The Marian antiphon:** DO's seasons are the same as 1960's, except on Holy Saturday, when *Regina caeli* begins (`easter-1:pentecost+5`).
- **The responsory in Passiontide:** DO's `Gloria1` omits the *Gloria Patri* unless the office is a saint's, or the Seven Sorrows' on the Friday after Passion Sunday (its rule "Gloria responsory"), whatever its rank: the date token `passiontideResponsory`. Checked against DO for the Lents of 1950, 2026, 2027, 2028 and 2038.
- **Checked:** for 1950, 2025, 2027, 2028 and 2100, the app's lookup in node matches DO day by day in the office, the preces, and the hymn's melody and doxology (DO's Advent and Ascension GABC differ from the app's only in a neume or two). In the browser, the page under 1960 is the same as before on 104 days through the year.

## Phase 4: checking (done)

- **The comparison:** the page, day by day, against DO's whole Divino Afflatu Compline, for 2026 and 2027 (730 days), none of which built the table.
  - DO's side runs `getordinarium` and `specials()` over `Ordinarium/Completorium.txt` for each day, loaded as `compline-office.pl` loads DO, with `&Gloria1` and `&Alleluia` resolved.
  - The page's side is the browser under `localStorage.rubrics = 'DA'`, setting each date and reading which chants and parts are shown.
  - What's compared:
    - the office line: title, rank, First Vespers;
    - *Alleluia* or *Laus tibi* after *Deus in adjutorium*;
    - the psalms, and their antiphon, intoned before and whole after;
    - the hymn's tone and doxology;
    - the chapter;
    - the responsory: Paschal, or Passiontide's without the *Gloria*;
    - the *Nunc dimittis* and its antiphon;
    - the preces, kneeling or not;
    - the Marian antiphon;
    - the Triduum's and All Souls' own Compline.
  - A multi-day run in one process has a trap besides `setupstring`'s cache: `canticum()` (horas.pl) declares `my $ant, $ant2;`, which declares only `$ant`, so the Easter octave's *Hæc dies* stays in the global `$ant2` for every later day. Each day resets it.
- **What differed, and is fixed:**
  - **The Saturday before Septuagesima:** its Compline follows the last Vespers with the *Alleluia*, so DO has *Laus tibi* after *Deus in adjutorium*; the app had *Alleluia*. Under DA the date token `septuagesimaEve` adds it. DO's 1960 has *Laus tibi* that evening too, so 1960's page has the same error; it's left, as 1960 isn't changed here.
  - **Maundy Thursday and Good Friday:** after the *Pater noster* said silently, DO says Psalm 50, *Miserere*, a little louder, and then the collect *Respice*, concluded silently with *Qui tecum* (`Tempora/Quad6-4`, `[Special Completorium]`, and `[Oratio Matutinum]`). The app had 1960's form, *Visita* with *Per Dominum*. Under DA, `index.html`'s `#miserere` now has the psalm, unpointed and without the *Gloria*, as the Triduum's other psalms.
    - The importer reads `index.html`'s psalm verses too (`app-latin.js`), and `psalter.js` has the section `50` with no ending.
    - The manifest has `index:respice-silent` and `index:qui-tecum-silent`.
    - The rubrics `triduum-pater-da` and `triduum-collect-da` are drafted in each language's `extra/<lang>/rubrics.txt` for the native readers to check.
- **The same as DO, but not in a way that tells the rubrics apart:** DO's plain text leaves the Paschal *alleluia* off *Salva nos* and the versicle *Custodi nos*, under 1960's rubrics too. The app's Paschal files have it, as the Liber Usualis does, and DO's own GABC for *Custodi nos*.
- **The hymn's quirks, looked at again:**
  - **The Christmas doxology on a Friday evening when DO commemorates Saturday's Office of Our Lady** is followed. Before 1955, a commemorated office that had a doxology of its own gave it to the hymns: DO's copy of *Cum nostra hac aetate* (`Help/Rubrics/1955.txt`, Tit. III, n. 5) is what took that away, as it did a commemorated feast's verse at Prime. DO applies it with the commemoration's rule `Doxology=Nat` (`hymni.pl`'s `doxology()`). Commune/C10, the Office of Our Lady on Saturday, has that rule, and the office begins at its First Vespers on Friday. The same rule gives the Christmas doxology when a feast of Our Lady is commemorated at Vespers: the Visitation on 1 July, the Presentation on a Saturday evening before a Sunday, the Queenship on Trinity Sunday.
  - **The Ascension's tone from the First Vespers of the 5th Sunday after Easter** isn't followed. DO decides the season of the hymn's tone (`get_tempus_id` in `SetupString.pl`) and of its doxology (`gettempora` in `horascommon.pl`) from the office's week, `$dayname[0]`, and the evening's weekday, `$dayofweek`. Its comment and its other weeks show the Ascension's season is meant from Wednesday's Vespers, the vigil's. But on Saturday evening `$dayname[0]` is already the Sunday's week, so the Saturday before the 5th Sunday passes the test for Wednesday and after. With the Sunday's office that gave the Ascension's tone and the Easter doxology. With a saint's, as Ss. Philip and James in 2005 and 2016, it gave the Ascension's doxology too.
  - **The same test gave Passiontide's tone in the week of Quinquagesima:** that week's name, `Quadp3`, passes neither the Septuagesima test after Tuesday nor Lent's `/^Quad(\d)/`, and falls through to `/^Quad/`, Passiontide. That gave Passiontide's tone on the Saturday evening before Quinquagesima, and from Ash Wednesday to Friday, on a feast too. It was missed in Phase 3.
  - So `compline-office.pl` takes the tone and the doxology on those evenings as on that week's Sunday, `$dayofweek` 0. Over the 69 kinds of year that changes the hymn on 327 days, and nothing else in the table. `Ascension-Easter` is no longer needed and is gone. 1960's `Seven-Dolors`, which DA had used on two of those evenings, is used only by 1960 now. The Saturday before the 5th Sunday is told from the Saturday after the Ascension by the date's own week, DO's `getweek`. That gives:
    - Paschal time's tone and the Easter doxology before the 5th Sunday;
    - the Sunday's tone on the Saturday before Quinquagesima;
    - from Ash Wednesday to Friday, the ferial tone, or a feast's own.
  - These are the Liber Usualis's seasons, as 1960's menu gives them from it: the Ascension's tone from the vigil, Lent's from the Saturday before the 1st Sunday of Lent, and the ordinary tones until then. The 1932 Liber Usualis, which the app follows for the Paschal tone (Phase 3), wasn't checked for these. DO's code is the same at `DO_COMMIT` and on its master as last fetched (2026-10-26).
- **All Souls' psalms:** the page labels all three, 122, 141 and 142. The first psalm's heading comes from its GABC's `user-notes` (`psalms/asd/psalm.gabc`, `Psalm 122.`), as Sunday's Psalm 4 does; `psalm-verses.html` has only the others'. Checked in Latin, side by side and in the translation. Fully notated, `psalm_full.gabc` has each of the three psalms with its own heading. Nothing to fix.
- **Checked:**
  - After the fixes, the page matches DO on all 730 days of 2026 and 2027. The only exceptions are the hymn's tone on the seven evenings where the app now departs from DO's seasons: in 2026, 14, 18, 19 and 20 February and 9 May; in 2027, 6 and 12 February.
  - The All Souls psalms showed as a difference only because the comparison counted the *Nunc dimittis* in the psalms.
  - Under 1960, the page is the same as before on every day of 2026: the feast line, and which chants, rubrics, texts, psalm verses and Marian antiphon it shows, compared against Phase 3's commit.
  - Under DA, the Miserere's translation shows beside the Latin and alone, with its heading translated, and the page has no horizontal scroll at 375px.
- **The note under the date** is gone: only the regional calendars don't follow Divino Afflatu, as Phase 2 says.
